**Project:** admissibility-beyond-minimal-surfaces

## 1) Core claims (what the paper explicitly asserts)

### C1 — Classical-saddle identification
Surface-minimization models of physical networks correspond to **classical saddle-point solutions** of a constrained geometric action (Nambu–Goto–type area minimization with a thickness/systole constraint), evaluated within a restricted topology class.

This is a structural identification, not an interpretive analogy.

---

### C2 — Structural incompleteness
Classical minimal surfaces, even when formulated rigorously and matched to data, are **structurally incapable** of deciding:

- topology selection among competing admissible configurations,
- refusal of smooth continuation under competing constraints,
- consistency of capacity constraints across scales.

This incompleteness follows directly from reliance on a single classical saddle under fixed constraints.

---

### C3 — Independence from empirical or numerical details
The incompleteness identified here is **not** due to:
- missing parameters,
- insufficient data resolution,
- suboptimal numerical methods,
- or the choice of specific biological systems.

It is a theory-level limitation that would persist even with perfect data and exact solvers.

---

### C4 — Necessity of an admissibility principle
Any theory that aims to decide the above questions must introduce an explicit notion of **admissibility**: a rule governing when smooth continuation is permitted and when topological reorganization is required.

The paper establishes *necessity*, not a unique implementation.

---

## 2) Explicit non-claims (what the paper does NOT assert)

### N1 — No replacement theory
This paper does **not** propose a replacement for surface minimization, Steiner optimization, or volume-based models.

Surface minimization is treated as a necessary and empirically successful component of physical network theory.

---

### N2 — No universal design principle
The paper does **not** claim:
- that all physical networks obey a single organizing principle,
- or that admissibility alone determines final network form.

Functional, developmental, and evolutionary constraints are acknowledged but not modeled.

---

### N3 — No new biological mechanism
The paper does **not** introduce or speculate about:
- biochemical signaling pathways,
- developmental control programs,
- evolutionary selection mechanisms.

All biological examples are used only to motivate theoretical structure.

---

### N4 — No empirical falsification
The paper does **not** attempt to falsify existing surface-minimization results.

All empirical successes of surface minimization are accepted as valid within their domain.

---

### N5 — No formal Σ₂ presentation
Although motivated by higher-order constraint logic, the paper does **not**:
- introduce Σ₂ formalism,
- define new symbolic systems,
- or require familiarity with Dual-Frame Theory.

Any such language remains internal or interpretive.

---

## 3) Boundary conditions on language and tone

To maintain credibility and impact:

- Use **neutral, field-native language** (classical saddle, admissibility, consistency).
- Avoid introducing new jargon unless unavoidable.
- Prefer “cannot decide” over “fails” or “breaks.”
- Frame extensions as **logical necessities**, not improvements.

---

## 4) Reviewer-misread prevention checklist

The paper must not be readable as claiming:

- “Surface minimization is wrong” ❌  
- “String theory explains biology” ❌  
- “This is a new universal network law” ❌  
- “Existing models are obsolete” ❌  

It **should** be readable as:

- “This clarifies what classical surface minimization does and does not decide” ✅  
- “This identifies a necessary next layer of theory” ✅  
- “This provides a principled boundary for optimization-based explanations” ✅

This document is considered *locked* when:

- No draft text violates N1–N5.
- Terminology matches the constraints above.
